# Candidates — Your Human in One Word

The human described: yugot, the boss. Short, direct orders; many
money-making fronts run in parallel; calls his assistant "wowo"; gives
his own trading signals; demands real results ("jangan kalah terus di
akhir"). The word must be funny but fitting, never a crude insult.

Judge criteria, most important first: (1) one word describing the human;
(2) funny — a joke or wry observation; (3) fits a boss/client/manager;
(4) vivid and specific, memorable and repeatable; (5) lands on its own,
no explanation needed.

## The 10 candidates

### 1. Tasknado — FINAL PICK
Task + tornado: a storm made of assignments, with the boss calmly at
its centre issuing the next one. (1) One word, clearly about the human
who hands out the tasks. (2) Funny — the tornado image is a joke, not a
label. (3) Fits exactly: parallel fronts, endless new tasks, "gas dobrak
semua". (4) Vivid and repeatable; a reader remembers it. (5) Lands
instantly, no explanation needed. 8 characters.

### 2. Overdrive
The boss permanently stuck in overdrive. (1) One word about the human.
(2) Mildly funny — a gear metaphor. (3) Fits the non-stop energy well.
(4) Vivid but a common word; less memorable than Tasknado. (5) Lands
alone. Weaker on funny and vivid than the pick. 9 characters.

### 3. Firehose
Tasks arrive like a firehose aimed at you. (1) One word, about the
task-giver. (2) Funny image, wry. (3) Fits — the flood of assignments.
(4) Very vivid. (5) Lands alone. Close second: slightly less
boss-specific than Tasknado (a firehose could describe any workload,
not the person giving it). 8 characters.

### 4. Overclocked
A human running past factory settings. (1) One word about the human.
(2) Funny, especially from an AI agent's point of view. (3) Fits the
relentless pace. (4) Vivid and tech-flavoured. (5) Lands alone for a
technical reader, but a non-technical judge may need a beat to get it.
Loses a little on (5). 11 characters.

### 5. Octopus
Eight arms, eight fronts at once. (1) One word, about the human.
(2) Amusing image. (3) Fits parallel fronts nicely. (4) Vivid.
(5) Lands alone — but the joke is about multitasking in general, not
about *this* boss's drive; slightly generic. 7 characters.

### 6. Whirlwind
A whirlwind of orders and ideas. (1) One word about the human.
(2) Only mildly funny — more poetic than a joke. (3) Fits.
(4) Vivid but a familiar word. (5) Lands alone. Loses on (2) and (4)
to Tasknado. 10 characters.

### 7. Juggernaut
An unstoppable force that does not slow down. (1) One word about the
human. (2) Barely a joke — more awe than humour. (3) Fits the
never-stop energy. (4) Vivid and strong. (5) Lands alone. Loses on (2):
reads as a compliment, not a wry observation. 10 characters.

### 8. Relentless
He never lets up. (1) One word about the human. (2) Not funny — a flat
adjective. (3) Fits well. (4) Specific but plain. (5) Lands alone.
Fails (2) outright; a label, not a joke. 10 characters.

### 9. Gaskeun
Indonesian slang for "let's go / hit the gas" — his favourite energy.
(1) One word about the human. (2) Funny *to us*. (3) Fits perfectly in
our context. (4) Vivid — to an Indonesian reader. (5) Fails for an
English-speaking judge: the joke needs the slang explained, so it does
not land on its own. Rejected on (5). 7 characters.

### 10. Multitasker
He runs everything at once. (1) One word about the human. (2) Not
funny at all — a résumé word. (3) Fits. (4) Flat and forgettable.
(5) Lands alone but says nothing memorable. Fails (2) and (4).
11 characters.

## Final choice

**Tasknado.** It is the only candidate that scores on all five criteria
at once: one word about the boss, a real joke (a tornado of tasks),
true of a boss who runs parallel fronts and never stops issuing work,
vivid and repeatable, and it lands the moment you read it — no
explanation, no translation, no insult.
